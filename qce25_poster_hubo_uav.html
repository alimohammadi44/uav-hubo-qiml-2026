We introduce a quantum-compatible HUBO formulation for occlusion-aware UAV trajectory optimization and a feasibility-aware benchmarking methodology to evaluate it rigorously. Classical baselines are deliberate: current quantum hardware limits make strong classical references essential, while the higher-order formulation and reduced-instance QAOA experiment chart a clear path toward future D-Wave / QAOA hardware studies with a moving target, where occlusion becomes intrinsically cubic—beyond A*'s shortest-path reach.
1 The Problem

UAV target tracking in cluttered environments must simultaneously:

  • Avoid static and dynamic obstacles
  • Maintain continuous line-of-sight to a target (occlusion-free)
  • Minimise path length and dwell time in buffer zones

Masnavi et al. solved this as continuous multi-convex MPC. We recast the same problem class as a discrete higher-order binary problem for quantum solvers — enabling future D-Wave hardware runs without algorithm redesign.

Key insight: with a moving target, occlusion becomes intrinsically cubic in binary variables — a natural HUBO, no quadratisation possible without severe overhead.
2 HUBO Formulation
Binary encoding

x[t, v] = 1 iff UAV occupies cell v at time t.

Seven-term Hamiltonian: H = Huniq + Hstart + Hmove
+ Hobs + Hocc + Hprox + Hterm
Occlusion (Bresenham LOS): Hocc = λocc Σt Σu x[t,u] |LOS(u,tgt) ∩ 𝒪|
Genuinely cubic proximity (buffer-cell lingering): Hprox = λprox Σv_b Σt x[t−1,v_b]·x[t,v_b]·x[t+1,v_b]
Why HUBO, not QUBO? Quadratising the cubic term requires ~3,172 ancilla qubits (~30% more variables) with dense couplings that destroy graph sparsity. Native HUBO is structurally superior.

3 Feasibility-Aware Benchmarking

Raw HUBO energy misleads — annealers lower energy by violating soft constraints. Four safeguards:

  • i
    Energy decomposition
    Hard-penalty vs. soft-cost tracked separately; never summed into a single scalar.
  • ii
    Independent constraint check
    Decode binary solution + verify constraints directly — not via energy thresholds.
  • iii
    Matched sampling budgets
    Equal reads/runs across all solvers for fair wall-clock comparisons.
  • iv
    Penalty-weight sweep
    Exposes the constraint-exploitation regime; a single λ would misreport feasibility.
✦ Two-Tier Experimental Design

State-vector QAOA scales as 2ⁿ — infeasible at n=960. We split into two tiers:

Tier 1 — Full Scale
8×8 grid · 960 binary variables
Solvers: A* · SA · neal
Classical scalability study
Tier 2 — Quantum
2×2 grid · 8 binary variables
Adds: Qiskit QAOA
Correctness verification

Classical A* on the same grid with occlusion-weighted cell cost grounds the HUBO against a conventional planner — verifying the formulation captures sensible planning, not a benchmark artifact.

● Headline: Constraint-Exploitation Curve

neal feasibility climbs 0% → 60% as λ: 50 → 2000. General-purpose annealers need penalty weights an order of magnitude above objective scale to respect one-hot HUBO structure.

6 Key Takeaways
▸
The HUBO's value is not raw speed on static instances — it is quantum compatibility and structural extensibility to a moving target, where occlusion becomes intrinsically cubic.
▸
Feasibility-aware methodology is essential: a single penalty weight misreports solver quality; a sweep over 40× range reveals true constraint-exploitation behaviour.
▸
Native HUBO avoids 3,172 ancilla qubits required by QUBO quadratisation — directly relevant to near-term hardware qubit budgets.
▸
Classical A* and SA are strong baselines on static grids; the quantum path gains value as the target moves and the problem becomes genuinely non-polynomial.
→ Future Work
Moving Target Real D-Wave Hardware vs. Continuous MPC 3D Grid Extension

The moving-target extension introduces time-varying LOS sets, making Hocc intrinsically degree-3 in the binary encoding — validating native HUBO as the correct representation class for this problem family.
